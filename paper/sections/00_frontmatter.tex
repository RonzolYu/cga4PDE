\begin{abstract}
We study the Chebyshev greedy algorithm (CGA) for convex nonlinear partial differential
equation energies using shallow \(\relu^k\) ridge functions, selected from finite candidate
pools with inexact coefficient solves. The one-step estimate separates four contributions:
coverage of the continuous dictionary by the pool, evaluation of the selection score,
radial stationarity in the active space, and the accuracy of the coefficient optimization.
Under an atomic source condition, directional smoothness, uniformly positive effective
selection, and relative error control, this estimate yields an \(O(m^{-1})\) energy bound.
For exact coefficient minimization, local Hessian transfer relates nonlinear selection
to weak orthogonal greedy approximation under relative-score, invariant-neighborhood,
and entropy conditions.
For the nested spaces generated by the algorithm, an approximate-source projection bound
keeps both the source remainder and the observed orthogonal increments. We illustrate how an exact energy expansion and explicit comparison conditions
convert projection estimates into nonlinear energy-error bounds. A separate power-type decrease hypothesis gives an explicit projection
bound on a finite index range. For standard \(p\)-Laplacian energies with \(p\geq2\), an energy
exponent \(\alpha\) yields natural-distance and Sobolev exponents \(\alpha/2\) and
\(\alpha/p\).
Eight one- and two-dimensional problems illustrate the estimates, including five comparisons with finite elements and random features at prescribed coefficient counts under a common evaluation protocol.
For the two quartic auxiliary models, the resulting bounds cover the computed energy errors over the calibration range; continuation with fixed constants tests the power-type decrease condition.
\end{abstract}
