\section{Equal-DOF baseline comparisons}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Comparison setup and scope}

Five problems use the same error evaluation: the one-dimensional linear and
cubic problems, the two-dimensional hyperbolic-sine problem, and the pure
\(p=4\) problems in one and two dimensions. Counts are
retained CGA/RFM coefficients and independent FEM coefficients after removing
the constant mode where required; equal counts imply neither equal numerical
rank nor equal computational work.

For these comparisons, CGA coefficients are reoptimized in the spaces spanned
by the first \(N\) functions selected in \Cref{sec:numerics}, with the selected
functions held fixed. FEM uses continuous Lagrange elements on uniform
one-dimensional meshes or structured triangular two-dimensional meshes.
Piecewise linear (P1) and cubic (P3) results appear in the main figures;
quadratic (P2) results are supplied in the supplementary material. RFM uses
nested normalized \(\relu^3\) ridges and ten prespecified seeds per width.
Sampling, coefficient optimization, quadrature, and convergence criteria
are specified in Supplement S4.

Figures connect computed results at powers of two and the final CGA coefficient
counts; FEM is plotted at its mesh counts. Each figure ends at the corresponding
CGA stopping width. Table values use \(N=256\) for the linear, cubic, and
hyperbolic-sine problems, and \(N=128\) and \(256\) for the one- and
two-dimensional pure \(p=4\) problems, respectively.
FEM table values interpolate log(error) against log(DOF) between valid computed results,
without extrapolation; P3 meshes do not bracket 512 in two dimensions.
These count-based choices precede inspection of the reported errors. Figures
contain neither common-grid interpolants nor theoretical reference lines.

\subsection{Evaluation and statistical samples}

Every method uses the same error definitions and validity criteria for each
metric: a successful solve, a finite nonnegative value, and a relative difference
of at most one percent between successive evaluation quadrature rules.
Negative computed energy gaps retain their sign and do not enter energy summaries;
an energy exclusion does not itself
exclude a stable Sobolev or \(V\) observation. Quadrature agreement is a
numerical diagnostic, not a rigorous enclosure.

RFM medians and interquartile ranges use each metric's valid observations.
The denominator remains ten; \emph{Valid} in \Cref{tab:baseline-terminal}
counts its Sobolev sample. Incomplete samples are conditional comparisons.
Supplement S3 gives convergence results, reasons for exclusions, and sample counts.

The two pure \(p=4\) problems use the relative componentwise
gradient \(L^4\) seminorm \(e_{\nabla,4}\) from \Cref{sec:numerics}, rather than
the relative full \(W^{1,4}\) norm. The natural \(V\)-distance is separate.

\Cref{tab:baseline-terminal} reports endpoint values and ratios.
A ratio above one favors CGA at that count; a ratio below one favors the
comparison method. Rankings depend on the problem, count, evaluation policy,
and valid sample. These data establish neither uniform dominance at every
DOF nor a ranking over excluded realizations.
For the one-dimensional pure \(p=4\) case at \(N=128\), the
ratio of cubic FEM error to CGA error is about 0.88, and the RFM-median/CGA ratio
is about 2.30. Thus the interpolated cubic FEM reference has a slightly smaller error than CGA
at this endpoint, while CGA is more accurate than the specified RFM median.

\subsection{Interpretation limits}

These comparisons concern the computed approximations for the five problems,
without establishing
runtime ordering, asymptotic complexity, or rankings over excluded realizations.
Energy gaps may remain unresolved while another metric is stable. S3--S4 give
convergence results, reasons for exclusions, interpolation intervals, and the
computed coefficients underlying the figures and tables.
